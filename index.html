About

Who I Am

I'm a PhD student in Quantitative & Computational Biology at Baylor College of Medicine, currently rotating through labs on cellular deconvolution and multi-omics integration in cancer genomics. Before starting my PhD, I worked as a Research Associate at Columbia University's Gertrude H. Sergievsky Center in statistical and functional genomics, with a focus on Alzheimer's disease.

My research interests span the tumor microenvironment, single-cell and spatial omics, cellular deconvolution and multi-omics integration, statistical genetics (fine-mapping, colocalization, xQTL mapping), and machine learning for heterogeneous biological data. I also have a solid foundation in biological signaling pathways and cellular mechanisms from hands-on wet lab training, and gained experience in causal inference and quantitative methods during my MPH in epidemiology.

I thrive in interdisciplinary environments, am a fast learner, and deeply value collaborative science — bridging dry and wet lab approaches in molecular and computational biology.
